> [!abstract] In brief
> An extremely violent boxer turned criminal, Loki bestowed his powers upon him, and he was transformed into the Absorbing Man, granting him powers to absorb any material and take on its properties. He is the on-again-off-again husband of Titania, and an enemy of Thor and Hulk.

## Origin

Carl Creel was a boxer using the ring name "Rocky Davis" in honor of his cousin, Rockwell Davis, whom he had sworn to protect. He became a heavyweight title contender before losing a key match to Battlin' Jack Murdock, and later became an extortionist who served time for assault. While in prison, Creel drank a potion that was made by Loki, which imbued him with the powers of matter absorption to battle Loki's enemies, Thor and Odin. Absorbing Man's first battle with Thor was heavily in favor of Absorbing Man. Mainly because if Thor were to touch Absorbing Man with his hammer, Mjolnir, Absorbing Man would be practically unstoppable. Thor, realizing that he could possibly lose the battle, took actions to outwit Absorbing Man, and banished him to space in the form of a gas.

## Powers

The Absorbing Man can alter the molecular structure of his body, his clothes, and his wrecking ball to mimic the properties of any material he touches. This process also gives him enhanced strength, endurance, and in most cases, resistance to injury. If the object touched holds some form of energy, such as a battery or a blast furnace, Creel absorbs these properties as well. The Absorbing Man can also absorb/duplicate the powers, strength, and durability of those within a certain distance, without the need for contact, as demonstrated in fights against Thor and the Hulk. He also has limited shape-changing abilities when absorbing certain objects, such as forming hammer-like fists when touching a hammer or growing to enormous size when copying items of massive power or strength. More recently, Creel has developed the ability to control the mind of another person, even over vast distances. He was shown to be able to "re-grow" his arm when it fell off (in his rocky form) by re-attaching it, then changing back to human form. Some of the substances he has absorbed are; Thor’s Uru hammer Mjolnir, Odin’s cosmic bolt, Hulk’s (gray) strength, Captain America’s shield, the Thing’s skin, titanium, bronze, Dazzler’s light, iron, diamond, helium, water, earth, granite, bricks, Ultron's metallic armor, the Sentry's energy, cinder-blocks and Quasar’s quantum bands. If his body is broken into pieces while in a non-human state he can mentally reassemble himself.

### Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D.

*Brian Patrick as Crusher Creel*

Carl "Crusher Creel" appears in Season 2 portrayed by Brian Patrick Wade of *Teen Wolf* and *The Big Bang Theory* fame. The MCU version of the character is not referred to by the "Absorbing Man" code name, and the source of his powers remains unrevealed. Nicknamed the Crusher, who used his secret abilities to pummel his opponents into submission. At some point, Creel moved on to murder, and was targeted for assassination by S.H.I.E.L.D., only to be rescued by double agent John Garrett and recruited by HYDRA. As part of HYDRA, Creel is tasked with recovering a rare alien obelisk from a U.S. military instillation, where he comes into conflict with a S.H.I.E.L.D. team sent by Phil Coulson. In the ensuing chaos, Creel kills Agents Isabelle Hartley and Idaho before managing to retrieve the obelisk. He is later subdued after the agents use a special device that leaves him inert, with the body turned over to the U.S. military for safekeeping.

Carl Creel, formerly a boxer known as Crusher Creel, was recruited by HYDRA for Project Destroyer of Worlds. An experiment with the Particle Infusion Chamber gave him the power to absorb the properties of anything he touched, making him the Absorbing Man. His death was faked by John Garrett, after which Creel was forced to steal the Obelisk for Daniel Whitehall. He was later captured by Phil Coulson’s team and, with help from Glenn Talbot, broke free from HYDRA’s mind control. Creel then became Talbot’s bodyguard when Talbot led the ATCU. After Talbot was injured in an assassination attempt by the Superior, Creel was manipulated again by Hale, who convinced him that S.H.I.E.L.D. was responsible. Creel absorbed Gravitonium to increase his power, which damaged his mind. When he discovered S.H.I.E.L.D. was not behind Talbot’s injuries, he helped Coulson and Talbot escape, but was hospitalized due to Gravitonium’s effects. Unbeknownst to Creel, Talbot’s mind was corrupted as well. Talbot tricked Creel into allowing himself to be absorbed and killed, so Talbot could take Creel’s powers for himself.

When S.H.I.E.L.D. discovered Carl Creel’s dangerous abilities, he was marked as an Index candidate and John Garrett was sent to eliminate him. Garrett, secretly a HYDRA agent, faked Creel’s death and recruited him as a brainwashed assassin. Under Daniel Whitehall’s command, Creel carried out missions without caring much about HYDRA’s goals. Creel interrupted a mercenary operation to steal a file from Roger Browning, killing Browning and effortlessly absorbing bullets while escaping despite heavy fire. Later, under Whitehall’s orders, Creel attempted to capture Glenn Talbot. Talbot, warned in advance, fought back with help from Melinda May. Creel, wielding his signature brass ball and chain and transforming his arm into brass for extra strength, nearly overwhelmed May, but she used her training to dodge and counterattack. Eventually, May and Skye subdued Creel—Skye electrocuted him with a taser projectile—and the U.S. Air Force arrested him. Creel surrendered without resistance, reverting his arm and raising his hands as Talbot was safely escorted away.

While imprisoned, Carl Creel was eventually restored to his human form after being frozen in stone by the Overkill Device. With time away from HYDRA’s influence, Creel finally broke free from the mental conditioning imposed by Daniel Whitehall. The fog in his mind lifted, and with it came regret. He began to reckon with the weight of his past—no longer a puppet, but a man trying to own his choices.

Glenn Talbot recognized the change. After months of observation and training, Talbot chose to trust him, recruiting Creel as his personal bodyguard. Creel had earned it. Talbot believed the man could be loyal, maybe even good. As Talbot stood at the airport, watching his wife walk away after Gideon Malick kidnapped their son, Creel remained close—silent but watchful, a soldier now without a leash.

During a scheduled meeting between Talbot and Phil Coulson, Creel trailed behind to keep an eye out for danger. Lincoln Campbell, wary of potential threats, noticed Creel’s presence and grew suspicious. Creel entered the parking lot behind the two men just as Coulson spotted him. Still viewing Creel as a HYDRA assassin, Coulson locked Talbot in a S.H.I.E.L.D. SUV, and May ordered Campbell to engage.

Before Creel could even explain, Campbell fired off a blast of electricity that struck him full-force. Instinctively, Creel reached for a car tire, absorbing its rubber texture to protect himself. Immune to further shocks, he threw Campbell across the lot into a garage door, then began advancing on Coulson.

Coulson responded with gunfire, unloading several rounds into Creel’s head, which barely slowed him. Campbell recovered and re-engaged, but May took advantage of the distraction—slamming a metal pipe into Creel’s head, baiting him into absorbing the metal. Once his body turned metallic, Campbell unleashed another electric surge. The electricity coursed through Creel’s now-conductive form, causing him to collapse as the heat built dangerously inside him. It was Coulson and May who stepped in just in time, ordering Campbell to stop before the energy killed him. Talbot, freed from the SUV, rushed to defend Creel, revealing that he was his bodyguard and had been protecting him the entire time.

With the misunderstanding resolved, Creel was taken to the Playground and placed in a Containment Module for observation. He was interviewed by Leo Fitz and Jemma Simmons, who wanted to understand the true nature of his abilities. Simmons, ever curious, pressed Creel about how he survived touching the Obelisk. He replied in short, grim answers—describing the experience as intensely painful, but offering little more.

Despite the distrust still brewing among S.H.I.E.L.D., Coulson struck a deal with Talbot: Creel would be allowed to join them on their next mission, doubling as Talbot’s bodyguard while secretly searching for Talbot’s kidnapped son, George.

Lance Hunter, still furious over Creel’s past actions—especially the deaths of Isabelle Hartley and Idaho—was openly hostile. But Creel, now seemingly a man on a different path, ignored the hatred. Aboard Zephyr One, he simply put on his headphones and tuned the world out, listening to his music while others questioned whether redemption was truly possible for the Absorbing Man.
